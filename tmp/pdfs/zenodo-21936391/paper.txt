Fused Triton Kernels for 4-Bit and Ternary Weight-Only Inference:
                 A TinyLlama-1.1B Case Study
                                              Anshuman Agrawal
                          University of Petroleum and Energy Studies, Dehradun, India



                                                     Abstract
         Autoregressive decoding repeatedly reads model weights from device memory, which makes weight
     compression useful on accelerators with limited memory capacity and bandwidth. This paper studies two
     custom Triton implementations for TinyLlama-1.1B: a zero-centered 4-bit format and a ternary format
     stored with two bits per weight. Both kernels load packed INT32 weights, decode them on chip, reconstruct
     FP16 tiles, and call tl.dot() with FP32 accumulation. On an NVIDIA T4, the 4-bit implementation
     reduced allocated inference memory from 2.21 to 1.05 GB and obtained a sampled WikiText-2 perplexity
     of 18.90, compared with 17.77 in FP16. In the tested software stack, it generated 21.61 tokens/s, versus
     20.56 tokens/s for bitsandbytes NF4 v0.49.2. The ternary representation used 2.125 effective bits per
     weight, including FP16 group scales, and reduced memory to 0.80 GB. Its quality, however, collapsed
     under naive groupwise absmean ternarization: sampled perplexity rose to 66,390 and generation became
     repetitive. A post-quantization affine adaptation that trained 788,480 parameters, about 0.07% of the
     nominal model size, for 500 steps reduced perplexity to 4,766 but did not restore useful text. These results
     are a case study of a simple ternary post-training baseline rather than a limit on ternary quantization in
     general. They provide an implementation-level reference for comparing more advanced activation-aware
     and optimization-based methods.
Keywords: large language models, weight quantization, Triton, GPU kernels, ternary quantization, post-
training quantization


1    Introduction
For batch-one autoregressive decoding, each generated token passes through the model’s linear layers while
reusing only a small activation state. The weights must still be read for every step. On memory-constrained
accelerators, this makes weight storage and memory traffic central deployment costs. An FP16 model requires
roughly two bytes per parameter before accounting for the key-value cache and runtime buffers, so even
comparatively small language models can leave little room for longer contexts or larger batches.
   Post-training quantization (PTQ) addresses this cost without changing the original training procedure.
Four-bit methods are now well established: GPTQ uses second-order information to reduce rounding error,
AWQ protects activation-salient weights, and NF4 uses a non-uniform codebook designed for approximately
normal weight distributions [1–3]. More elaborate schemes such as AQLM and QuIP# also show that useful
models can be compressed below three bits per weight when the quantizer is designed around vector structure,
incoherence, or learned codebooks [4, 5].
   Ternary models restrict weights to {−1, 0, +1}. BitNet and BitNet b1.58 demonstrated that this constraint
can work when it is part of training [6–8]. The question studied here is narrower. We ask what happens
when a conventional FP16 checkpoint is converted with a direct groupwise quantizer and executed through a
custom Triton path, without the activation-aware optimization, layer reconstruction, or extended retraining
used by stronger ternary methods.
   This paper makes four contributions:
• We implement weight-only W4A16 and 2-bit-packed ternary W2A16 linear layers in Triton, with on-chip
  unpacking and dequantization.

• We separate the measured result from broader claims about ternary PTQ and relate the failure to newer
  methods that use asymmetric grids, activation-aware fitting, reordering, and learned modulation.


2     Related Work
2.1    Four-Bit and Extreme PTQ
GPTQ quantizes weights by using approximate second-order information to control the output error introduced
by rounding [1]. AWQ instead uses activation statistics to identify and protect important weight channels [2].
QLoRA introduced NF4 and double quantization for memory-efficient fine-tuning, and NF4 is also widely
used as a weight-only inference format in bitsandbytes [3]. At lower bit widths, AQLM represents weights
through additive codebooks, while QuIP# combines incoherence processing with lattice codebooks and
fine-tuning [4, 5]. These methods are relevant because they show that storage width alone does not determine
model quality; the quantization objective and representation matter just as much.

2.2    Ternary Training and Ternary PTQ
BitNet and BitNet b1.58 train transformer weights under binary or ternary constraints rather than applying
those constraints after training [6, 7]. BitNet b1.58 2B4T later demonstrated a native ternary model at the
two-billion-parameter scale [8]. TernaryLLM uses learnable ternarization and feature distillation to recover
information lost at W1.58A16 precision [9].
    Recent work has also moved ternarization into the post-training setting. PT2 -LLM uses asymmetric ternary
fitting, activation-aware grid alignment, and structural reordering [10]. TWLA combines ternary weights
with low-bit activations through asymmetric fitting, rotations, and mixed-precision activation treatment [11].
CAT-Q uses learnable modulation and softened ternarization on a small calibration set [12]. These results
mean that a failed symmetric absmean baseline should not be read as evidence that ternary PTQ is generally
infeasible. Our contribution is an implementation and failure analysis of that simple baseline on a small
model.

2.3    Low-Precision GPU Kernels
Triton provides a Python-level language for tiled GPU kernels and allows non-standard packing schemes to
be expressed without writing architecture-specific CUDA assembly [13]. Our kernels use FP16 activations
and reconstruct FP16 weight tiles before tl.dot(). This differs from Microsoft’s public BitNet GPU kernel,
which targets W2A8 inference and uses packed integer decoding together with the dp4a instruction [14]. The
distinction matters: ternary storage does not by itself imply ternary arithmetic on current GPUs.

3.4    Fused Triton Linear Layer
Each kernel processes the output in two-dimensional tiles. For every step along K, it loads packed INT32
words and FP16 scales from global memory, decodes the selected 4-bit or 2-bit fields with shifts and masks,
reconstructs an FP16 weight tile in on-chip memory, and calls tl.dot() against the FP16 activation tile.
Partial products accumulate in FP32 and are cast to FP16 at the output. The 4-bit path decodes eight
values per packed word; the ternary path decodes sixteen. Five candidate tile configurations are supplied to
@triton.autotune, which selects a configuration for each matrix shape.
    This W2A16 design reduces stored weight bytes but still performs a floating-point dot product after
reconstruction. Tensor Core use depends on the matrix shape and the compiler’s lowering of tl.dot(); the
implementation does not assume a native three-state matrix instruction.

3.5    Model Integration
Linear4Bit and LinearTernary are drop-in nn.Module replacements for eligible nn.Linear layers. Packed
weights and scales are registered as non-trainable buffers. A model-tree traversal replaces linear layers while
leaving embeddings, the language-model head, and excluded projection layers in FP16. The original FP16
weight is released after replacement to reduce conversion-time memory pressure.

3.6    Post-Quantization Affine Adaptation
To test whether a small amount of optimization can recover ternary quality, each ternary linear output y is
followed by a learned per-output scale a and shift b:

                                              y ′ = a ⊙ y + b.                                          (9)

The packed ternary weights remain frozen. Only a and b are trained with next-token cross-entropy on
WikiText-2 training text. This adds 788,480 trainable FP32 parameters, approximately 0.07% of a nominal
1.1-billion-parameter model. We use a learning rate of 10−5 and run 500 optimization steps.


4     Experimental Setup
4.1    Hardware, Software, and Model
All measurements were made on one NVIDIA T4 exposed through Google Colab. The board has 16 GB of
GDDR6 memory and 300 GB/s nominal memory bandwidth; the runtime exposed approximately 15 GB
to the process [18]. The reported software stack was Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.10, Triton 3.2, CUDA 12.8,
Hugging Face Transformers 5.5.4, and bitsandbytes 0.49.2.
    The target checkpoint was TinyLlama/TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat-v1.0, a 1.1-billion-parameter model based
on the Llama architecture [16]. We compared four configurations: FP16, Triton 4-bit, Triton ternary, and
bitsandbytes NF4 v0.49.2.
    The bitsandbytes result is tied to that exact version and setup. Version 0.50.0, released after these
measurements, introduced new fused 4-bit dequantization-and-GEMM kernels, including kernels for Turing
GPUs [15]. The numbers in this paper should therefore not be treated as a benchmark of current bitsandbytes
releases.

4.2    Metrics
Allocated memory. We reset CUDA peak-memory statistics after quantization, run a forward pass,
and report torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated(). This measures allocations tracked by PyTorch during
inference; it is not a board-level measurement of every byte reserved by the CUDA runtime.

Decode throughput. Throughput is measured during greedy generation of 128 new tokens. Two warm-up
runs precede five timed runs. Each configuration is evaluated in three independent subprocess trials, and
the table reports the mean and standard deviation across those trials. CUDA synchronization is performed
around timed regions.

Language-model quality. Perplexity is estimated as the exponential of mean cross-entropy over 50
examples sampled from the WikiText-2 test split [17]. Because this is a sampled evaluation rather than
the standard full-corpus protocol, the absolute values should not be compared directly with full WikiText-2
results reported elsewhere. Four fixed prompts covering code generation, factual explanation, optimization,
and quantization were also inspected qualitatively.

    The 5.1% difference should not be attributed to kernel fusion alone. The comparison changes the
quantization grid, storage format, module implementation, and kernel-dispatch path at the same time. We
did not collect profiler traces or implement an unfused baseline using the same zero-centered 4-bit quantizer,
so the experiment does not isolate the cause of the throughput difference.
    Across the four qualitative prompts, the 4-bit model produced coherent responses with no obvious failure
of the kind seen in the ternary model. This observation is informal and is not a substitute for downstream
task evaluation.

5.3    Ternary Result
The ternary implementation reduced allocated memory to 0.80 GB, a 63.8% reduction relative to FP16. Its
throughput, 21.55 tokens/s, was effectively the same as the 4-bit path within the observed trial variation.
The quality result was very different: sampled perplexity increased to 66,390.29, and generation collapsed
into repetitive token fragments and punctuation patterns.
    Repeated evaluations returned the same perplexity because the quantizer and evaluation path were
deterministic. That repeatability does not test robustness to alternative calibration samples, thresholds,
group sizes, or model checkpoints.

5.4    Storage Cost
The table reports the actual fixed-width representation used by the kernels. Calling this implementation
“1.58-bit” would be inaccurate: each ternary value occupies two physical bits, and the FP16 group scale raises
the effective cost to 2.125 bits per weight.

5.5    Standalone Kernel Measurements
The custom 4-bit kernel was slower than cuBLAS for every standalone shape tested. This is compatible
with the model-level comparison: the cuBLAS baseline multiplies FP16 weights, whereas the model-level
bitsandbytes comparison uses a different compressed representation and software path. End-to-end speed
cannot be inferred from the standalone matrix multiplication table alone.

5.6    Post-Quantization Affine Adaptation
The adaptation reduced sampled perplexity by a factor of 13.93, from 66,390.29 to 4,766.00. The adapted
model was still about 268 times worse than the FP16 baseline on the same sampled evaluation, and qualitative
generation remained incoherent. The result shows that per-output scales and shifts can correct part of
the accumulated distribution error, but they are not expressive enough to reconstruct the input-dependent
structure removed by this ternary quantizer.


6     Discussion
6.1    What the Ternary Experiment Establishes
The experiment establishes a specific failure mode: groupwise absmean ternarization with a fixed symmetric
three-state grid breaks TinyLlama-1.1B under the W2A16 setup used here. It does not establish that all
ternary PTQ methods fail. PT2 -LLM, TWLA, and CAT-Q use asymmetric grids, activation-aware objectives,
reordering, rotations, softened optimization, or learned modulation to address precisely the kinds of asymmetry
and outlier sensitivity ignored by a direct absmean projection [10–12].
    Our measurements do not identify a single causal layer or statistic. A plausible interpretation, consistent
with that later work, is that the simple quantizer introduces structured errors that compound across layers.
Testing that interpretation requires layerwise output error, activation-weighted reconstruction error, ternary
occupancy, and mixed-precision rescue experiments. Those analyses were not part of the present study.




                                                      7
6.2    What the Affine Adaptation Shows
A learned output scale and shift can change the mean and magnitude of each output channel, but it cannot
restore the original input-channel-specific weight pattern. The strong early reduction in perplexity shows that
some of the failure is correctable after quantization. The remaining gap shows that this correction family is
too limited for deployment-quality recovery on this checkpoint. More expressive alternatives include low-rank
adapters, blockwise reconstruction, knowledge distillation, or full quantization-aware fine-tuning.

6.3    Kernel Implications
The ternary format achieves its memory reduction through packing, not through a native ternary matrix
instruction. In our W2A16 implementation, values are decoded and reconstructed as FP16 before the dot
product. Integer W2A8 kernels such as Microsoft’s public BitNet implementation follow a different route
and use integer dot-product instructions [14]. Future performance work should therefore compare complete
arithmetic paths, not only nominal weight bit widths.
    For the 4-bit path, the end-to-end result against bitsandbytes v0.49.2 is encouraging but narrow. A current
comparison should include bitsandbytes 0.50.0 or later, record the selected kernels, separate prefill from
decode, and profile memory traffic and launch overhead. It should also include an unfused implementation of
the same quantizer so that fusion can be measured independently of format differences.


7     Limitations and Future Work
This study uses one 1.1B chat checkpoint, one T4 GPU, one group size, and one simple thresholding rule.
The quality evaluation is based on 50 WikiText-2 examples and four qualitative prompts rather than the full
corpus and a standard downstream benchmark suite. Throughput is reported for a single fixed generation
setup; prompt length, batch size, prefill latency, and longer-context behavior are not swept. The bitsandbytes
baseline is v0.49.2 and does not represent the later fused 4-bit implementation. We also did not collect profiler
traces, test multiple random calibration subsets, or evaluate layerwise sensitivity.
    The next experiments should vary group size and ternary thresholds, quantize one layer at a time, measure
layer-output cosine similarity and mean-squared error, and retain sensitive layers at four or sixteen bits. The
benchmark should be repeated on at least one additional 1B–3B checkpoint and on a current consumer GPU.
A stronger comparison would implement PT2 -LLM, CAT-Q, or another modern ternary PTQ method using
the same model and evaluation protocol. On the systems side, prefill and decode should be reported separately,
and the W2A16 path should be compared with an integer-activation W2A8 design. Quantization-aware
fine-tuning is also a natural extension; Gemma 3 provides a practical example of short QAT schedules for
4-bit checkpoints [19].


8     Conclusion
We implemented fused Triton linear layers for a zero-centered 4-bit format and a 2-bit-packed ternary
format, then evaluated them on TinyLlama-1.1B and an NVIDIA T4. The 4-bit path cut allocated inference
memory by 52.5% and kept sampled perplexity close to FP16. Under the tested bitsandbytes 0.49.2 setup, it
also achieved a modest decode-throughput advantage, although it used more memory than NF4 and was
slower than FP16. The ternary path reduced memory by 63.8% but failed under naive groupwise absmean
quantization. Training a small affine correction reduced the error substantially without restoring useful
generation.
    The main conclusion is deliberately narrow: a simple symmetric ternary projection is not a viable drop-in
replacement for TinyLlama-1.1B in this setup. The result is best used as a baseline for methods that optimize
the ternary grid, account for activations and outliers, or retrain the model under low-precision constraints.
